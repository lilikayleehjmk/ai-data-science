Objective/Question:

How can we reduce the time and manual labor of collecting data on snow hardness? Specifically, can snow layer location and thickness predict snow hardness?

Importance:

 Snow hardness is traditionally measured by hand, which is inefficient, but the electronic equipment able to replace manual labor is specialized, heavy, and expensive. The lack of efficient collection of snow hardness data is increasingly dangerous as global warming softens snow and risks wildlife populations. Hard crusts allow heavy animals to walk on top of the snow, while softer layers cause them to sink into the snow, thus wasting energy and limiting access to food. As weather patterns change, collecting data regarding snow hardness efficiently is vital for specialists to model winter survival for alpine species and make efforts towards ecosystem conservation. 

Data Types:

I expect to gather observational field data from Dall sheep tracks in Wrangell Mountains of Alaska and analyze factors like snow hardness, snow depth, and layer thickness.
