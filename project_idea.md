How can we predict and prevent the affect of softening snow on alpine species? Specifically, how has snow hardness changed and affected the Dall sheep in the Wrangell Mountains of Alaska?
    The Dall sheep population has decreased by 30% since 1990s and global warming is melting alpine snow at a rapid rate. Hard crusts allow heavy animals to walk on top of the snow, while softer layers cause them to sink into the snow, thus wasting energy and limiting access to food. As weather patterns change, understanding layer thresholds is vital for specialists to model winter survival for alpine species and make efforts towards ecosystem conservation and wildlife management.
I expect to gather observational field data from Dall sheep tracks in Wrangell Mountains of Alaska and analyze factors like snow hardness, snow depth, and layer thickness.
